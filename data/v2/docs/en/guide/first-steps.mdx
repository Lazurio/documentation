Draft a meeting agenda, then save the parts you want to reuse next week. The first prompt includes all the notes you need. If you use your own notes, choose material you can share in that Chat. New to working with agents? Read the [short introduction](/en/guide/start-here/).

## 1. Brief your first task and review the result

Open Chat in your Environment. If you work with several Organizations there, tell the agent which one the draft is for.

```text
We have half an hour to pick a training date and decide who will prepare the materials. I still need to find out whether the supplier has sent a quote.
Draft an agenda and list anything I should check before the meeting. Use only these notes. Don't send anything or create a calendar event.
```

**Example agenda**

- Before the meeting, check whether the supplier has sent the quote.
- Spend the first 15 minutes choosing a training date.
- Use the remaining 15 minutes to agree on who will prepare the materials and by when.

Read through the draft. Does it match your notes? The date and the person responsible are still open decisions. If an item needs more time, adjust it in the same thread.

<details class="lz-learning-chat"><summary>How to adjust the timing</summary><p><strong>Example feedback:</strong> Five minutes is plenty to pick a date. Leave the other 25 for dividing up the work.</p></details>

## 2. Explain one recurring procedure

If you prepare a meeting like this every week, save the rule in your shared Knowledgebase. In the same Chat, try:

```text
We check the facts before a meeting and use our time together for decisions.
Look in our Organization's Knowledgebase for an existing meeting preparation guide. If we have one, show me where this rule would fit. Otherwise, draft a short guide. Ask me about anything you still need to know.
Show me the draft, where it would go and who could read it. Don't publish it yet.
```

Check whether the procedure would work for another meeting. Once the draft is ready, the person with permission to publish it in your Organization can approve publication.

## 3. Use the approved procedure in a new task

Once the procedure has been approved and published, start a new Chat in the same Organization. Try a meeting with a different topic:

```text
Find our approved meeting preparation guide in the Knowledgebase and give me the link. If you can't find or access it, tell me and wait.
This time we need to choose a workshop venue. We don't yet know how many people are coming. Use the guide to suggest what to check beforehand and what to decide together. Don't send anything to the team.
```

Open the guide and compare it with the draft. Knowing the headcount beforehand will help the team choose a venue. If the agent couldn't find the guide, check which Organization published it and who has access.

## When to start a new thread

Stay in the same thread when refining the same result. Start a new task for
a different goal: a meeting agenda and an unrelated document analysis can
run in parallel. Give the new task its materials; do not assume it knows
the neighboring chat. Do not have two agents edit the same document without
coordination.

## Outlook, SharePoint and other tools

First verify an available **approved connection**, the correct account and
the access scope. Being signed in through your regular browser is not proof
that the agent has access.

```text
I need to work with Outlook. Check whether an approved connection is
available in this environment and tell me which account and operations
you can use. Do not read or change anything yet. If the connection is
missing, explain the next step; do not switch to another service or browser
control without discussing it with me.
```

After verification, try a small read-only task:

```text
In the specified SharePoint folder [link], find the document [name].
Summarize it in five points and include the source. Search only that folder.
Do not edit or share anything. If you lack access, stop and explain why.
```

Replace brackets with actual details. These are task instructions, not
automatic access setup. See more examples in [Connect your tools](/en/guide/tool-connections/)
and [working in the browser](/en/guide/work-tips/).

## When a response is slow or misses the point

If your tool offers a reasoning setting, try a lower level for simple tasks
and a higher one for demanding analysis. This does not guarantee speed or
accuracy. Controls vary by application, and delays can have other causes.

```text
Summarize what you have verified, what you are waiting for and what is missing.
Propose the smallest next step. Do not repeat the same unsuccessful approach.
```

When the result misses the point, describe the difference: “I need a decision,
not a transcript of the notes.” If the problem persists, send support the
application name, a short brief, expected and actual results, and a sanitized
screenshot of the error. Do not include passwords, tokens or sensitive materials.

## Your first two weeks, without a separate training project

- **Day one:** finish a small task and improve the draft with specific feedback.
- **Week one:** capture one recurring procedure in Knowledgebase and have it approved.
- **Week two:** use the procedure in a new task and fill gaps exposed by real work.

Adjust the pace to your work; these are suggestions, not deadlines.
